# Claim-level novelty matrix for Milestone 1.5

Audit date: 2026-08-09

This matrix records what can and cannot be claimed after the fresh primary-source
search. `Clearly covered` means that the central claim is already demonstrated in
the literature and must not be presented as a contribution. `Strongly threatened`
means that a close mechanism exists, even if the exact code-switched benchmark or
retriever pair differs. `Possible gap` means only that no exact match was found;
it is not evidence that the claim is novel.

| ID | Candidate claim | Primary evidence | Status | Exact overlap | Remaining distinction | Conditions for defensibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Natural code-switching can degrade retrieval relative to the original query. | [CSR-L/CS-MTEB](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.636/); [Retrievability of Code Mixed Microblogs](https://doras.dcu.ie/23399/) | Clearly covered | The robustness problem and mixed-language retrieval setting are established. | This project can add a source-safe development measurement, not a new problem claim. | State C1 only as replication/characterization on the two development resources; report Delta_CS with uncertainty. |
| C2 | BM25 and dense retrievers fail on different queries and therefore have complementarity. | [MoR](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.601/); [FIRE CMIR findings](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4173/T3-1.pdf) | Clearly covered | Per-query comparative wins, oracle routing, and complementary lexical/neural systems are prior art. | The project measures the pattern for fixed BM25/Qwen on natural zh-en queries. | Treat disagreement/oracle headroom as a diagnostic motivation only; do not claim discovery of complementarity. |
| C3 | Combining BM25 and dense retrieval improves code-mixed retrieval. | [ContrastiveMix](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-short.17/); [FIRE dense-sparse RRF](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4173/T3-7.pdf); [Decoding Benglish](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4173/T3-9.pdf) | Clearly covered | Code-mixed BM25+dense reranking, RRF, and fixed hybrid baselines already exist. | No combination or score fusion is being implemented in Milestone 1.5. | Do not claim a hybrid contribution; if later used, register it only as an established baseline. |
| C4 | CMI, switch ratio, language entropy, or related code-switch structure can decide retrieval behavior. | [SETU-RAG CMI router](https://digitalcommons.isical.ac.in/masters-dissertations/458/); [When Does Mixing Help?](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1455/); [Code-Mixed Probes](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.307/) | Strongly threatened | Structure/mixing-driven routing or analysis is already present, and the project’s Milestone 1 correlations are weak/inconsistent. | A carefully controlled test could ask whether a structure signal transfers, but the mechanism is not new. | Reject structure-only alpha/gating. Any future analysis must compare against non-structure QPP signals and cannot use CMI as a novelty claim. |
| C5 | A query-specific sparse/dense weight or alpha is a novel contribution. | [Query-Adaptive Hybrid Search](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-4990/8/4/91); [QuDAR](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1791/); [From Romanised](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4173/T3-3.pdf) | Clearly covered | Learned/query-specific alpha, confidence weighting, and code-mixed hybrid weighting are directly documented. | A natural zh-en transfer evaluation with fixed BM25/Qwen would be a different evaluation setting. | Do not present alpha/fusion as novel; any later alpha is a registered comparative baseline only. |
| C6 | A query-level router can choose sparse, dense, or hybrid retrieval. | [Arabzadeh et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/3459637.3482159); [MoR](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.601/); [RouterRetriever](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/33306) | Clearly covered | Query-level strategy selection, mixture routing, and expert routing are established. | The exact target could be fixed BM25 versus fixed Qwen under natural code-switching, with no retriever training. | Only study this as a replication/transfer question; compare to generic selectors and avoid an invention claim. |
| C7 | Unlabeled confidence, margin, coherence, or query difficulty can choose the better retriever. | [QuDAR](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1791/); [Coherence-based QPP](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/328868/); [Arabzadeh et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/3459637.3482159) | Strongly threatened | Margin/confidence, embedding coherence, query features, and label-free model selection are all prior mechanisms. | The relative BM25-versus-Qwen target on naturally code-switched queries and source-safe transfer was not found as an exact match. | Frame any follow-up as testing calibration/transfer of existing signals, with fixed candidate signals and strong generic baselines; no claim that confidence/QPP is new. |
| C8 | Combining code-switch structure with retriever behavior can estimate relative retriever reliability. | [Query-Adaptive Hybrid Search](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-4990/8/4/91); [QuDAR](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1791/); [SETU-RAG](https://digitalcommons.isical.ac.in/masters-dissertations/458/) | Possible gap, high risk | The components—adaptive weighting, confidence, complementarity, and structure-aware routing—are individually and partly jointly covered. | No inspected source matched the complete natural-CS, fixed-BM25/fixed-Qwen, relative-reliability, cross-dataset Delta_CS evaluation. | Keep only as a narrowly scoped empirical calibration/transfer RQ. Do not call the feature combination a novel method; pre-register the signals, splits, baselines, and stopping rule. |
| C9 | Selecting the more reliable retriever can mitigate code-switching degradation. | [QuDAR](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1791/); [Arabzadeh et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/3459637.3482159); [FIRE CMIR systems](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4173/) | Partially covered, high risk | Generic routing, query-adaptive weighting, RRF, and code-mixed hybrid retrieval already seek this outcome. | A strict no-training, hard-selection study of fixed retrievers under natural CS and cross-dataset transfer is narrower than the cited systems. | Claim only an evaluation of whether existing unlabeled signals transfer. Success requires outperforming pre-registered baselines on held-out development data and preserving original-query performance; no final-test tuning. |

## Bottom line

C1–C3 are established findings or diagnostics. C4–C7 are directly or strongly
threatened by prior art and by the weak Milestone 1 structure correlations. C8 and
C9 can support at most a high-risk, conditional empirical question about transfer
and calibration of existing signals. They do not justify implementing an adaptive
alpha, fusion rule, CMI router, or new retrieval model in Milestone 1.5.
