# Milestone 2.6 fresh literature search log

Search date: 2026-08-09 (Asia/Bangkok)  
Review scope: code-switched IR, sparse/dense retriever selection, query
performance prediction, selective prediction/abstention, retriever
disagreement/coverage, multi-source reliability, calibration, and cross-domain
generalization.  The search was literature-only. No repository retrieval,
encoding, evaluation, GPU, ArguAna, or CSR-L operation was performed.

## Sources and databases searched

The review used the public web interfaces of the following primary or
publisher-controlled sources:

- ACL Anthology, including ACL/NAACL/EMNLP/EACL/LREC-COLING and 2026 volumes.
- ACM/SIGIR, ICTIR, CIKM, and publisher DOI records.
- AAAI proceedings.
- NeurIPS proceedings.
- Springer/ECIR records and author-hosted accepted manuscripts.
- arXiv records only where the primary venue record was unavailable or as an
  accessible version of a verified paper.
- MDPI journal record for the 2026 query-adaptive hybrid paper.
- CEUR-WS FIRE 2025 CMIR proceedings.
- University/institutional repositories for the Glasgow QPP paper and the
  SETU-RAG dissertation.
- Microsoft Research publication records for the pre-retrieval QPP paper.

Search-result snippets were used for discovery only. The abstract and, for the
closest mechanisms, the method/setup text or official publisher metadata were
checked before inclusion. Non-primary blogs, review pages, ResearchGate pages,
and commercial summaries were not used as decisive evidence.

## Inclusion rules

Included works had to satisfy at least one of the following: (i) directly study
code-switched or code-mixed retrieval; (ii) select, route, mix, or calibrate
sparse/dense retrievers at query or corpus level; (iii) estimate query/ranker
performance without relevance labels; (iv) use selective prediction,
abstention, or explicit risk/coverage objectives relevant to retrieval or
knowledge access; or (v) study reliability transfer, source reliability, or
cross-domain uncertainty for retrieval systems.  Preference was given to
peer-reviewed 2024–2026 primary papers and to papers with a concrete method,
dataset, and evaluation setup.

## Exclusion rules

Excluded were generic code-switching papers with no retrieval or reliability
implication, purely architectural MoE routing with no retrieval/model-choice
connection, application papers whose routing was unrelated to retrieval
selection, surveys used as if they were method evidence, and web commentary.
The survey/benchmark papers that were retained are marked as such in
`prior_work.csv` and were used for landscape or threat evidence rather than as
method claims.

## Deduplication and resolution

Duplicate arXiv/publisher records were collapsed to one work, with the
peer-reviewed venue retained when available and the arXiv URL kept only as an
access copy.  QuDAR, CSR-L/CS-MTEB, MoR, RouterRetriever, Query-Adaptive Hybrid
Search, RA-RAG, LARMOR, and the FIRE systems were separately retained because
they are distinct mechanisms or settings.  The final CSV contains 36 unique
works.

## Closest-work verification

The following were checked beyond a title-only match: QuDAR's abstract and
official ACL metadata confirm top-1/top-2 margin confidence and dynamic
sparse/dense/original/expanded-query weighting; Query-Adaptive Hybrid Search's
official MDPI record confirms query-driven alpha prediction, BM25 plus dense
fusion, antagonist negatives, 101 alpha bins, and MLDR/MIRACL evaluation; MoR's
ACL record confirms per-query mixture/weighting of sparse and dense retrievers;
RouterRetriever's AAAI record confirms query-specific expert routing on BEIR;
RA-RAG's ACL record confirms source-reliability estimation, selective retrieval,
and weighted majority voting; CSR-L/CS-MTEB's ACL record confirms human
annotation, sparse/dense/late-interaction evaluation, and code-switching
degradation; Glasgow's institutional record confirms dense QPP predictors and
query-type instability; and the FIRE PDFs were inspected for their reported
BM25+dense, RRF, and reranking setups.

## Access limitations and unresolved items

Some ACM full texts were not openly readable through the search interface, so
official DOI metadata and author-hosted or arXiv copies were used.  OpenReview
pages intermittently presented a browser-verification page; those records were
not used as decisive evidence where an official venue or ACL record was
available.  The 2026 literature is unusually recent; inclusion means the
official proceedings or version-of-record page existed on the search date, not
that every paper has a long citation history.  No unresolved item was needed to
decide the audit: the strongest collisions are independently documented by
official ACL, AAAI, MDPI, ACM, and EMNLP records.

## Search conclusion

The search did not identify a clean, unoccupied mechanism-level gap among H1,
H2, or H3.  It did identify a possible narrow evaluation distinction—whether
existing unlabeled signals transfer to relative BM25-versus-dense reliability
under benchmark-provided code-switched variants and source-safe domain transfer—
but that distinction is an evaluation setting, not a new method, and is directly
threatened by current query-wise adaptive retrieval, QPP, selective-risk, and
retriever-mixture work.  The decision is therefore a novelty NO-GO.
